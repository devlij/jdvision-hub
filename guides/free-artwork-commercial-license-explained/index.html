Free Artwork for Commercial Use: Licensing Explained

October 8, 2026 · 7 min read

"Free download" and "free for commercial use" are not the same thing. Here's what the common licenses actually allow — and what to check before putting free visuals on your product, website, or marketing.

What "Free for Commercial Use" Means

You can use the image to make money: on product packaging, in advertisements, on a monetized website, in a book you sell. No royalties, no per-use fees.

Creative Commons Zero (CC0): The Gold Standard

CC0 is the most permissive license available. It means:

  • Use for any purpose, including commercial
  • No attribution required
  • Modify freely
  • No permission needed

Sources like Unsplash (mostly), Pexels, and Pixabay use CC0 or CC0-equivalent licenses.

What to Watch Out For

  • "Free download" ≠ "free commercial use." Some sites let you download free but restrict commercial use to paid plans.
  • Attribution requirements. Creative Commons BY licenses require credit. CC0 does not.
  • Model and property releases. Even with a permissive license, images with recognizable people or private property can carry additional legal considerations.
  • "Editorial use only." Some stock licenses prohibit commercial use entirely.

Jason D's Vision License

All images on Jason D's Vision are free for personal and commercial use with no attribution required — equivalent to CC0 in practical terms. Use them on packaging, in hotels, in books, on websites, anywhere.

The bottom lineBefore using any "free" image commercially, verify: (1) commercial use is allowed, (2) whether attribution is required, (3) if there are restrictions on modification or redistribution. When in doubt, CC0 and attribution-free sources are the safest bet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Creative Commons Zero (CC0) allow?

Everything. Use, modify, distribute, sell — for any purpose, no attribution, no permission needed.

Can I use free artwork on product packaging?

Yes, if the license allows commercial use. CC0 and Jason D's Vision's license both cover packaging.
